Timing feels: the frame before the frame

In a garden dash — and in animation — the hop you see is sold by the quiet beat before it. Anticipation is not a pose library trick; it is a decision about when the audience believes you.

  1. Hold one beat early. Before a jump, squash or settle for 2–4 frames (at 24fps). Viewers read intent before motion.
  2. Ease out harder than you ease in. Leaving the ground can stay snappy; landing should linger a touch so weight reads.
  3. Offset the companion. Pip (or a secondary prop) hopping a half-beat later makes the lead feel intentional, not stiff.
  4. Cut detail while timing. Block silhouette and spacing first. Color and shaders come after the beat lands.
